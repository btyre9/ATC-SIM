How to control an aircraft

First click an aircraft strip on the right. Then click MIC and speak; normal pauses are fine. The mic closes about 2½ seconds after you stop talking, or click MIC again to finish right away. You do not need to say the callsign again. If Chrome hears a word incorrectly, edit the text in the command bar and press SEND. You can still say a callsign and instruction together if you prefer.

Procedures

  • Arrivals (amber) enter over the arrival fix on their strip at 10,000 to 14,000 ft and 250 kt. Descend them, vector them to join the localizer at 30° or less, from at or below the glideslope, then clear them for the ILS.
  • Once an arrival is established on the localizer, say "contact tower". Landing without a handoff costs points.
  • Departures (blue) wait at the hold-short line beside the runway. Tower (or you, in manual departure mode) lines them up and waits, then clears them for takeoff. They fly their SID: runway heading to 1,000 ft, then direct to their departure fix, climbing to 5,000. Climb them to 10,000 ft or higher.
  • Once a departure is through 7,000 ft, climbing to at least 10,000, and on course to its fix, say "contact center". Leaving the dashed 40 NM boundary without a handoff costs points.
  • On the ILS an arrival holds your heading until it reaches the localizer (or takes a 20° intercept if your heading never would), tracks the centerline, joins the 3° glideslope from below or descends to it from above, and lands. If it is not on the localizer by 1 NM out, or is still well off the centerline or high at 1.5 NM, it goes around.
  • Go-arounds fly the missed approach: runway heading, climb to about 3,000 ft above the field (4,000 ft at Atlanta, Charlotte and Detroit, 3,000 at San Diego), 210 kt. They hold that until you give new instructions, and come back to your frequency.
  • Runways: one aircraft on a runway at a time, counting both ends (09 and 27 are one runway). Don't line up or clear a departure with an aircraft on the runway or an arrival inside about 2 minutes; tower waits for that. Entering a runway with an aircraft on it or an arrival inside 1 NM is a runway incursion (−200). An arrival finding the runway occupied goes around.
  • Runway changes: when the wind swings, departures taxi to the new runway and approach clearances to the old one are cancelled (except arrivals established inside 5 NM). Re-clear those arrivals.
  • Speed: 250 kt maximum below 10,000 ft (14 CFR 91.117); 160 kt minimum until established on final.
  • Separation: 3 NM or 1,000 ft; 2.5 NM between aircraft on the same final.
  • Expect runway tells an arrival which runway to plan for. On its own navigation it flies to that runway's final (a real approach fix 8 to 16 NM out, or a point 12 NM out) and turns onto the runway course; on vectors it keeps your heading. It still needs an approach clearance.
  • Holding: "hold northeast of fix, left turns" puts the pattern on that side of the fix; "hold at fix" holds on the far side from the airport with right turns. The aircraft flies the FAA entry for its heading (direct, teardrop or parallel), 1-minute legs (1½ above 14,000 ft) and slows to the holding limit (200 kt to 6,000 ft, 230 kt to 14,000). Altitude and speed changes keep it holding; a heading, direct or approach clearance takes it out. Vector it to a 30° intercept before clearing the ILS.
  • Direct works with any fix on the scope; triangles are real FAA arrival and departure fixes.

In this simulator, “flight level three thousand” means 3,000 feet and “flight level twelve thousand” means 12,000 feet. “Flight level three zero zero” still means 30,000 feet. Say heading digits separately: “two seven zero” for 270°. ILS clearance requires an aircraft within 30 nautical miles of the runway.

Pilot voice

Each aircraft keeps its assigned voice throughout the session.
